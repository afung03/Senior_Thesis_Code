>> 1980 STUDY DESCRIPTION

       THE NES/CPS AMERICAN NATIONAL ELECTION STUDY, 1980, WAS
       CONDUCTED BY THE CENTER FOR POLITICAL STUDIES OF THE
       INSTITUTE FOR SOCIAL RESEARCH, THE UNIVERSITY OF MICHIGAN,
       UNDER THE OVERALL DIRECTION OF WARREN E. MILLER. MARIA ELENA
       SANCHEZ WAS DIRECTOR OF STUDIES IN 1980.  THIS IS THE
       SIXTEENTH IN A SERIES OF STUDIES OF AMERICAN NATIONAL
       ELECTIONS PRODUCED BY THE POLITICAL BEHAVIOR PROGRAM OF THE
       SURVEY  RESEARCH CENTER AND THE CENTER FOR POLITICAL
       STUDIES, AND THE SECOND STUDY TO BE CONDUCTED UNDER THE
       AUSPICES OF A NATIONAL SCIENCE FOUNDATION GRANT (NO.
       SOC77-08885) WHICH PROVIDES LONG-TERM SUPPORT FOR NATIONAL
       ELECTION STUDIES.

       THE 1980 ELECTION STUDY WAS AN AMBITIOUS PROJECT CONSISTING
       OF EIGHT INTEGRATED SURVEY DATA COLLECTIONS SCHEDULED TO
       OCCUR AT STRATEGICALLY CHOSEN PERIODS IN THE COURSE OF THE
       CALENDER YEAR.  THE INTRODUCTORY SECTION "DESIGN OF THE
       NES/CPS AMERICAN NATIONAL ELECTION STUDY, 1980" PROVIDES
       DETAILS ABOUT THE OVERALL STUDY DESIGN.  THE FORMULATION OF
       THE DESIGN STARTED IN SUMMER, 1978, WITH A WORKSHOP THAT
       BROUGHT TOGETHER THE CPS PROJECT STAFF, MEMBERS OF THE NES
       BOARD OF OVERSEERS, AND A GROUP OF SCHOLARS REPRESENTING
       BOARD INTERESTS.  THE GROUP OF SCHOLARS INVITED TO
       PARTICIPATE IN THE 1978 WORKSHOP WERE:  JOHN H. ALDRICH,
       RICHARD A. BRODY, STEVEN H. CHAFFEE, JACK DENNIS, LUTZ
       ERBRING, JOHN H. KESSEL, DONALD R. KINDER, RICHARD G. NIEMI,
       AND HERBERT F. WEISBERG.  PHILIP E. CONVERSE, M. KENT
       JENNINGS AND ARTHUR H. MILLER FROM THE CENTER FOR POLITICAL
       STUDIES ALSO ATTENDED THE MEETINGS.  A STIMULUS MEMORANDUM
       ON THE 1980 DESIGN OPTIONS WAS MAILED TO THE SCHOLARLY
       COMMUNITY IN THE AFTERMATH OF THE WORKSHOP TO ELICIT FURTHER
       IDEAS ON DESIGN AND CONTENT. IN THE MONTHS THAT FOLLOWED,
       THE STUDY DESIGN WAS FURTHER SPECIFIED AND REVISED TO
       ACHIEVE ITS PRESENT FORM.

       AS PART OF RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT EFFORTS SPONSORED BY THE
       NES BOARD, SEVERAL CONFERENCES AND WORKSHOPS WERE HELD IN
       1977-1979 TO EXPLORE INNOVATION IN CONTENT AND MEASUREMENT
       FOR THE 1980 STUDY.  BOARD MEMBERS DURING THE 1980 PLANNING
       PHASE INCLUDE:  HEINZ EULAU, STANFORD UNIVERSITY; RICHARD F.
       FENNO, JR., UNIVERSITY OF ROCHESTER; JOHN E. JACKSON,
       UNIVERSITY OF PENNSYLVANIA; DAVID R. MAYHEW, YALE
       UNIVERSITY; WARREN E. MILLER, UNIVERSITY OF MICHIGAN,
       EX-OFFICIO; BENJAMIN I. PAGE, UNIVERSITY OF CHICAGO; MILDRED
       A. SCHWARTZ, UNIVERSITY OF ILLINOIS, CHICAGO CIRCLE; DAVID
       O. SEARS, UNIVERSITY OF CALIFORNIA, LOS ANGELES; J. MERRILL
       SHANKS, UNIVERSITY OF CALIFORNIA, BERKELEY; AND JOHN
       SPRAGUE, WASHINGTON UNIVERSITY.

       FOUR AREAS WERE TARGETED FOR SPECIAL ATTENTION:  NEW
       MEASUREMENT OF PARTY IDENTIFICATION, THE MEASUREMENT OF
       VOTER ATTITUDES CONCERNING ISSUES OF PUBLIC POLICY, NEW
       CONTENT CONERNING PUBLIC PERCEPTIONS OF AND
       RESPONSES TO POLITICAL LEADERSHIP, AND THE EXPLORATION OF
       SOCIAL NETWORKS IN THE CRYSTALLIZATION OF VOTE CHOICE.
       SUBSTANTIVE CONSIDERATIONS FROM THE VARIOUS CONFERENCES AND
       WORKSHOPS, AS WELL AS SUGGESTIONS MADE BY SCHOLARS WHO
       SUBMITTED MEMORANDA IN CONNECTION WITH THESE MEETINGS,
       CULMINATED IN THE PLANNING AND EXECUTION OF NATIONAL PILOT
       STUDY IN SPRING, 1979.  RESULTS FROM THE PILOT STUDY HAVE
       INFORMED SUBSTANTIVE DECISIONS AND INNOVATIONS IN THE 1980
       STUDY CONTENTS.  THE MAJOR CONTRIBUTORS TO THE PILOT STUDY
       WERE:  ROBERT P. ABELSON, JOHN H. ALDRICH, RICHARD A. BRODY,
       STEVEN H. CHAFFEE, PETER CLARKE, JACK DENNIS, LUTZ ERBRING,
       HEINZ EULAU, SUSAN FISKE, JOHN E. JACKSON, RICHARD S. KATZ,
       DONALD R. KINDER, ARTHUR H. MILLER, RICHARD G. NIEMI, DAVID
       W. ROHDE, DAVID O. SEARS, J. MERRILL SHANKS, HERBERT F.
       WEISBERG, AND RICHARD R. LAU.

       THE NES BOARD ESTABLISHED A 1980 PRESIDENTIAL ELECTIONS
       COMMITTEE CONSISTING OF THREE BOARD MEMBERS (MERRILL SHANKS,
       CHAIRMAN; JOHN JACKSON, DAVID SEARS) AND THREE SCHOLARS WHO
       PARTICIPATED IN THE R & D WORK (RICHARD A. BRODY, JACK
       DENNIS, DONALD R. KINDER).  THIS COMMITTEE, ALONG WITH THE
       CPS PROJECT STAFF, WAS RESPONSIBLE FOR THE PLANNING OF THE
       YEAR-LONG STUDY.









>> 1980 - DESIGN OF THE NES/CPS AMERICAN NATIONAL ELECTION STUDY

       EVOLUTION OF THE NES/CPS AMERICAN NATIONAL ELECTION STUDY,
       1980 DESIGN RESULTED IN THE SPECIFICATION OF EIGHT DISCRETE
       DATA COLLECTIONS. ALL INTERVIEWS WERE TAKEN WITH SAMPLES OF
       CITIZENS OF VOTING AGE AS OF NOVEMBER 4, 1980, LIVING WITHIN
       THE COTERMINOUS UNITED STATES.  AS IN 1978, CONGRESSIONAL
       DISTRICTS CONSTITUTE THE SAMPLING PRIMARY AREAS, AND THE
       WASHINGTON, D.C. AREA IS NOT REPRESENTED IN THE NATIONAL
       SAMPLE. ALL OF THE EIGHT DATA COLLECTIONS ARE AVAILABLE IN
       ONE LARGE FILE, REFERRED TO AS THE INTEGRATED FILE.

       FOUR OF THE DATA COLLECTIONS INVOLVE A YEAR-LONG PANEL,
       WHICH HAS BEEN RELEASED SEPARATELY BY THE CONSORTIUM AS THE
       MAJOR PANEL FILE.

       A FIFTH COMPONENT OF THE DESIGN  CONSISTED OF AN INDEPENDENT
       CROSS- SECTION SAMPLE OF 965 CASES (DESIGNATED IN MOST
       DESCRIPTIONS OF THE 1980 COLLECTION AS C-1) INTERVIEWED IN
       THE MIDST OF THE PRIMARY SEASON DURING THE THREE-WEEK
       "WINDOW" FOLLOWING THE EARLY APRIL PRIMARIES. THEY WERE
       REINTERVIEWED FOLLOWING THE ELECTION WITH A TELEPHONE
       INTERVIEW. 818 CASES MAKE UP THIS REINTERVIEW (C-4), THE
       SIXTH COMPONENT OF THE STUDY.  C-1 AND C-4 ARE AVAILABLE
       ONLY AS PART OF THE INTEGRATED FILE, AND ARE NOT RELEASED
       SEPARATELY (ALTHOUGH ARRANGEMENTS MAY BE MADE WITH
       CONSORTIUM MEMBER SERVICES TO SUBSET THE INTEGRATED FILE IF
       JUST THESE COLLECTIONS ARE DESIRED).

       THE SEVENTH AND EIGHTH COMPONENTS ARE THE TRADITIONAL
       PRE-POST ELECTION TIME SERIES SURVEYS, DOCUMENTED IN THIS
       CODEBOOK.  THE PRE-ELECTION INTERVIEW (C-3) WAS TAKEN WITH
       WAS TAKEN WITH 1614 CASES (APPROXIMATELY 15 PER CONGRESSIONAL
       DISTRICT.  THE EIGHTH COMPONENT (C3PO) CONSISTS OF 1408
       POST-ELECTION REINTERVIEWS WITH THE PRE-ELECTION SAMPLE.
       THE REINTERVIEW RATE IS 87 PERCENT.

       THE LAST DATA COLLECTION TOOK PLACE IN JANUARY-FEBRUARY,
       1981, WHEN THE VOTE VALIDATION STUDY WAS CONDUCTED.
       INTERVIEWERS WENT TO ELECTORAL OFFICES THROUGHOUT THE NATION
       TO ESTABLISH WHETHER THE RESPONDENTS WERE REGISTERED IN 1980
       AND, IF REGISTERED, WHETHER THEY HAD VISITED THE POLLS TO
       VOTE ON ELECTION DAY.  1980 ELECTION RESULTS WERE ALSO
       COLLECTED AT THIS TIME FOR THE PRECINCTS REPRESENTED IN THE
       SAMPLE, AS WELL AS SAMPLE BALLOTS FOR THE DISTRICTS AND A
       COPY OF ELECTORAL ELIGIBILITY RULES.  THESE DATA ARE
       INCLUDED IN ALL THREE OF THE CONSORTIUM RELEASES DISCUSSED
       ABOVE; THE INTEGRATED FILE, THE MAJOR PANEL FILE AND THE
       PRE-AND POST ELECTION SURVEYS FILE.

       THE SAMPLE SELECTIONS FOR THE MAJOR PANEL FILE WERE
       INITIALLY DIVIDED INTO FIRST-HALF AND SECOND-HALF SAMPLE.
       THIS SAMPLE DIFFERENTIATION WAS IGNORED WHEN P-1 AND P-2
       WENT INTO THE FIELD IN FAVOR OF MAXIMIZING THE EFFICIENCY OF
       THESE DATA COLLECTIONS.  HOWEVER, THE THIRD PANEL INTERVIEW
       (P-3) WAS ADMINISTERED AS TWO RANDOM HALVES; THE FIRST HALF
       WAS INTERVIEWED IN EARLY SEPTEMBER,  THE SECOND IN LATE
       SEPTEMBER.

       THE TRADITIONAL PRE ELECTION INTERVIEW (C-3) WAS ALSO
       SEGMENTED, WITH INTERVIEWS IN EARLY SEPTEMBER (WITH THE
       FIRST HALF OF P-3), LATE SEPTEMBER (WITH THE SECOND HALF OF
       P-3), IN THE FIRST PART OF OCTOBER, AND IN LATE OCTOBER.
       THIS SEGMENTATION OF THE C-3 SAMPLE PERMITS THE COMPLETE
       OVERLAP OF P-3 WITH THE NEW CROSS-SECTIONS IN SEPTEMBER,
       PERMITTING AN ASSESSMENT OF CONTAMINATION INTRODUCED INTO
       THE PANEL AS A RESULT OF PRIOR INTERVIEWS.  ASSUMING ALSO
       MINIMAL BIAS AS A RESULT OF PANEL MORTALITY, IT PERMITS THE
       PYRAMIDING OF P-3 AND THE FIRST TWO COMPONENTS OF C-3 TO
       FORM A LARGER SEPTEMBER SAMPLE.







>> 1980 SAMPLING INFORMATION

       THE STUDY EMPLOYS A SAMPLING FRAME OF CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICT
       PREVIOUSLY USED TO OBTAIN THE SAMPLE FOR THE NES/CPS
       AMERICAN NATIONAL ELECTION STUDY, 1978.  THESE 108
       CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICTS COMPRISE THE PRIMARY SAMPLING AREAS.
       THEY WERE SELECTED IN THE FIRST STAGE OF THE MULTI-AREA
       PROBABILITY SAMPLE DEVELOPED IN 1978.  AT THAT TIME, THE
       UNIVERSE OF 432 ELIGIBLE CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICTS WAS DIVIDED
       INTO 108 STRATA, EACH CONTAINING FOUR CONGRESSIONAL
       DISTRICTS OF ROUGHLY COMPARABLE CHARACTERISTICS THAT
       INCLUDED, INSOFAR AS POSSIBLE, HOMOGENEITY WITH  RESPECT TO
       GEOGRAPHIC REGION, STATE, URBANIZATION AND RECENT VOTING
       BEHAVIOR. THESE STRATA ARE NUMBERED 1 THROUGH 108 IN VARIABLE
       827.  FROM EACH OF THE 108 STRATA, ONE DISTRICT WAS
       THEN SELECTED WITH PROBABILITY OF SELECTION IN PROPORTION TO
       THE 1975 ESTIMATED POPULATION TO YIELD THE 108 CONGRESSIONAL
       DISTRICTS IN THE SAMPLING FRAME.

       IN THE NEXT STAGES OF SELECTION, SAMPLE AREAS WERE OBTAINED
       WITHIN EACH OF THE SAMPLE DISTRICTS.  WHEN THE CONGRESSIONAL
       DISTRICT WAS PART OF A MAJOR CITY, A SAMPLE OF BLOCKS WAS
       DISTRIBUTED THROUGHOUT THE DISTRICT.  IN THE CASE OF
       DISTRICTS THAT COMPRISED SEVERAL COUNTIES, GENERALLY, ONE OR
       TWO COUNTIES WERE SELECTED, AND, WITHIN THE SAMPLE COUNTIES,
       ABOUT 10 OR 12 CITY OR TOWN BLOCKS AND SEGMENTS OF RURAL
       AREAS WERE DRAWN.  A SELECTION OF HOUSING UNITS FOR THE
       CURRENT STUDY WAS MADE FROM LISTS OF HOUSING UNITS WITHIN
       SUCH SAMPLE BLOCKS AND SEGMENTS.

       AT THE OVERALL RATE OF  1:11,200, A SAMPLE OF APPROXIMATELY
       7,500 HOUSING UNITS WAS DRAWN FOR USE THROUGHOUT THE
       YEAR-LONG MONITORING CYCLE.  THESE ARE THE DWELLINGS WHERE
       INTERVIEWERS CALLED AT DESIGNATED TIMES TO ATTEMPT
       INTERVIEWS WITH ONE OBJECTIVELY SELECTED ELIGIBLE PERSON.
       THE SAMPLE WAS DESIGNED TO BE REPRESENTATIVE OF REGIONS, AS
       DEFINED BY THE FOUR-FOLD CENSUS BUREAU REGION CLASSIFICATION.

       THE C-3 SAMPLE SELECTIONS WERE SCIENTIFICALLY DIVIDED INTO
       FOUR NATIONAL SUBSETS ADMINISTERED DURING SEPTEMBER AND
       OCTOBER IN CONSECUTIVE TWO-WEEK INTERVIEWING PERIODS.  MOST
       OF THE INTERVIEWS WERE COMPLETED DURING THE TARGETED TIME
       FRAME.  LATE INTERVIEWS FOR ANY GIVEN ADMINISTRATION PERIOD
       WERE GENERALLY COMPLETED IN THE WEEK IMMEDIATELY FOLLOWING
       THE TARGETED PERIOD, THE SO-CALLED "GRACE PERIOD."  SOME

       INTERVIEWS, HOWEVER, WERE COMPLETED LATER THAN THIS FOR A
       VARIETY OF REASONS, INVOLVING AMONG OTHER THINGS, THE
       RESPONDENT'S UNAVAILABILITY AT THE APPROPRIATE TIME. SEE
       REFNO   48 FOR A DETAILED BREAKDOWN.

            LATE INTERVIEWS FOR TWO PRIMARY AREAS (CONGRESSIONAL
       DISTRICTS TX03 AND CA29) REPRESENT UNUSUAL AND UNFORTUNATE
       CIRCUMSTANCES. MID-WAY THROUGH THE C3PO POST-ELECTION SURVEY,
       THE FIELD OFFICE VERIFIED THAT ALL C3 PRE-ELECTION INTERVIEWS
       IN THESE TWO AREAS HAD BEEN FABRICATED BY THE AREA
       INTERVIEWER.  TO RECOVER SOME OF THE MISSED PRE-ELECTION
       INFORMATION, INCLUDING DEMOGRAPHIC DATA, A SHORTENED PRE-
       ELECTION INTERVIEW WAS CONDUCTED WITH THE LEGITIMATE
       RESPONDENTS AT THE TIME THEY WERE CONTACTED FOR THE POST-
       ELECTION ADMINISTRATION.

            THE NUMBER OF C3 SAMPLE SELECTIONS IN TX03 WAS MUCH
       LARGER THAN CA29; FIELD WORK IN THE TEXAS DISTRICT COULD NOT
       HAVE BEEN SUCCESSFULLY COMPLETED UNLESS THE INTERVIEWING
       LOAD WAS REDUCED.  ACCORDINGLY, A RANDOM HALF-SAMPLE WAS
       DRAWN FROM THE ORIGINAL TX03 SAMPLE SELECTIONS FOR C3 AND
       ONLY THESE RESPONDENTS WERE CONTACTED AS POST-ELECTION TIME.
       WEIGHTING TO COMPENSATE FOR THIS SUBSAMPLING DONE IN TX03
       WAS HANDLED BY DUPLICATING THE DATA RECORDS OF THE
       RESPONDENTS IN THE TARGETED HALF-SAMPLE.  THIS PROCEDURE MADE
       IT UNNECESSARY TO RESORT TO A WEIGHT VARIABLE FOR CORRECTIONS
       PURPOSES.

             THE PRE-ELECTION  CASE ID'S OF THE RESPONDENTS IN TX03
       AND CA29 AND THE CASE ID'S FOR THE DUPLICATED TX03 RECORDS
       IN THE C3 SAMPLE ARE AS FOLLOWS:

                                  C3 CASE ID

       TX03                       1701-1715  (RANDOM HALF)
                                  1751-1765  (DUPLICATE CASES)

>> 1980 CONTENTS OF THE PRE-POST ELECTION FILE

          AS WITH OTHER PARTS OF THE AMERICAN NATIONAL ELECTION
       STUDY, 1980, THE PRE-AND POST ELECTION SURVEYS FILE HAS A
       VERY HEAVY EMPHASIS ON CANDIDATE EVALUATION AND VOTE CHOICE.
       RESPONDENTS ARE ASKED HOW CANDIDATES MAKE THEM FEEL, WHAT
       WORDS DESCRIBE CANDIDATES, HOW CANDIDATES RATE ON THE
       FEELING THERMOMETER, WHAT THEY LIKE AND DISLIKE ABOUT EACH
       AND REASONS FOR VOTING FOR OR AGAINST EACH MAJOR PRESIDENTIAL
       CANDIDATE.

          IN ADDITION TO NOW-STANDARD QUESTIONS ON PARTY IDENTIFI-
       CATION, RESPONDENTS ARE PROBED EXTENSIVELY ABOUT WHAT THEY
       MEAN WHEN THEY SAY THEY ARE REPUBLICANS, DEMOCRATS OR
       INDEPENDENTS.  RESPONDENTS ARE ALSO ASKED ABOUT THEIR
       ATTENTION TO MEDIA, POLITICAL ACTIVISM, CAMPAIGN CONTRIBU-
       TIONS, AND WHETHER THEY WERE CONTACTED IN ANY WAY BY THE
       HOUSE, SENATE, GUBERNATORIAL OR PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION
       CAMPAIGNS.

       THIS FILE INCLUDES A FULL RANGE OF OCCUPATION AND EDUCATION
       MEASURES.  USERS SHOULD READ THE VARIABLE DESCRIPTION LIST
       SECTION RELATING TO THESE MEASURES WITH CARE, AND SHOULD
       ALSO REFER TO NOTE 13.  THIS SECTION DIFFERS IN ORGANIZATION
       FROM PREVIOUS NATIONAL ELECTION STUDIES, AND FROM THE WAY
       THESE VARIABLES WERE ORGANIZED IN THE PREVIOUS CONSORTIUM
       RELEASE.

       INDIVIDUAL VOTER RECORD VALIDATION DATA (VARIABLES
       801197-841208) REPORTS THE EFFORT TO VALIDATE THE RESPONSES
       ABOUT REGISTRATION AND VOTING BEHAVIOR.  PERSONAL VISITS
       WERE MADE BY MEMBERS OF THE SRC FIELD STAFF DURING JANUARY
       AND FEBRUARY 1981 TO THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT OFFICES CHARGED
       WITH THE RESPONSIBILITY FOR MAINTAINING REGISTRATION AND
       VOTING RECORDS IN THE 108 SAMPLE DISTRICTS.  TELEPHONE CALLS
       WERE MADE BY CPS STAFF TO THOSE PLACES OUTSIDE THE STUDY
       SAMPLE WHEN A RESPONDENT CLAIMED TO BE REGISTERED IN SUCH AN
       AREA.

       INCLUDED ALSO IN THIS  RELEASE OF THE PRE-AND POST ELECTION
       SURVEYS ARE A SET OF CONTEXTUAL DATA WHICH WERE COLLECTED
       FROM VARIOUS SOURCES AND THEN ADDED TO EACH RESPONDENT'S TO
       RECORD.  THESE CONTEXTUAL DATA VARIABLES INCLUDE ELECTION
       RETURNS FOR THE YEARS 1972-1980, INTEREST GROUP RATINGS OF
       INCUMBENTS AND FEDERAL ELECTION COMMISSION DATA ON
       CAMPAIGN EXPENDITURE.  THE CONTEXTUAL DATA IS DOCUMENTED
       IN THE APPENDICES.

       IN ANALYZING THE CONTEXTUAL DATA, USERS SHOULD BE AWARE
       THAT, ALTHOUGH THE DATA IS PRESENT IN EVERY RECORD, IT IS
       STATE, COUNTY OR CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICT LEVEL DATA.
       THEREFORE, VALUES WILL BE THE SAME FOR ALL RESPONDENTS

      FROM THE SAME STATE, IN THE CASE OF STATE LEVEL DATA,
       OR CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICT, IN THE CASE OF DISTRICT
       LEVEL DATA.

>> 1980 NOTES ON THE ADMINISTRATION OF THE INTERVIEWS

       SPECIAL INFORMATION: POST-ELECTION FEELING THERMOMETERS

          NAMES APPEARING TWICE ON THE FEELING THERMOMETER (SUCH AS
       IN THE CASE OF STATES WHERE THE INCUMBENT REPRESENTATIVE,
       ASKED ABOUT IN Q.G5W, WAS ALSO A CANDIDATE FOR THE U.S.
       SENATE, ASKED ABOUT IN Q.G5M/N) WERE ASKED ONLY ONCE AT THE
       FIRST LOCATION.  THE RESPONSE TO THE FIRST ENTRY WAS THEN
       POST-EDITED INTO THE SECOND REFERENCE.
          IN SELECTED RACES WHERE AN INDEPENDENT/MINOR PARTY
       CANDIDATE WAS PERCEIVED TO BE A POTENTIAL THREAT TO ONE OF
       THE TWO MAJOR PARTY CANDIDATES, THIS CANDIDATE WAS INCLUDED
       IN THE DESCRIPTION OF THE RACE.  THAT IS, THE TYPE OF RACE
       WOULD BE CODED 13, 19, 23, 29, 56-59, 66-69, OR 76-79 IN
       Q.AA5, Q.AA6, AND/OR Q.AA8.  THESE INDEPENDENT/MINOR PARTY
       CANDIDATES WERE REFERENCED IN THE FEELING THERMOMETERS IN
       Q.G5O AND Q.G5V.

       SPECIAL INFORMATION: NEW YORK STATE

          IN THE RACE FOR SENATE IN NEW YORK, JACOB JAVITS, THE
       REPUBLICAN INCUMBENT, LOST THE REPUBLICAN PRIMARY BUT
       RECEIVED THE NOMINATION OF THE LIBERAL PARTY AND RAN UNDER
       THIS PARTY BANNER.  THE NEW YORK STATE SENATE RACE IS CODED
       AS "69" IN Q.AA6 (REF. NO 629) - "REPUBLICAN INCUMBENT NOT
       RUNNING WITH DEMOCRATIC, REPUBLICAN AND OTHER CANDIDATES".
       THROUGHOUT THE QUESTIONNAIRE, JAVITS IS REFERENCED AS AN
       INDEPENDENT/ MINOR PARTY CANDIDATE.
          LIBERAL, CONSERVATIVE, AND INDEPENDENT CANDIDATES RUNNING
       IN NEW YORK HAVE BEEN INCLUDED IN THE CANDIDATE LIST FOR
       SEVEN CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICTS: 03, 13, 19, 22, 26, 27, AND
       30.  IN CASES WHERE THE DEMOCRATIC OR REPUBLICAN CANDIDATES
       ALSO HAVE THE SUPPORT OF THE LIBERAL OR CONSERVATIVE
       PARTIES,  THEY ARE CONSIDERED THE DEMOCRATIC OR REPUBLICAN
       CANDIDATE.  WHERE THE INCLUSION OF THESE CANDIDATES RESULTED
       IN A THREE-WAY RACE, THE CONSERVATIVE, LIBERAL OR
       INDEPENDENT CANDIDATE WAS REFERRED TO IN Q.G5V.

       SPECIAL INFORMATION: MAINE

          IN MAINE, Q.G5S ("THE OTHER SENATE INCUMBENT WHOSE TERM
       IS NOT UP") REFERS TO EDMUND MUSKIE, DESPITE THE FACT THAT
       HE HAD LEFT OFFICE THE PRECEDING MAY TO BECOME SECRETARY OF
       STATE.  THIS DECISION WAS MADE BECAUSE IT WAS FELT THAT
       MUSKIE WAS THE "ELECTED" SENATOR AND HAD HELD THE OFFICE FOR
       A LONGER PERIOD OF TIME.

       SPECIAL INFORMATION:  WEST VIRGINIA

          IN WEST VIRGINIA CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICT 3, THE LATE JOHN
       M. SLACK WAS TREATED AS THE INCUMBENT REPRESENTATIVE EVEN
       THOUGH HE DIED IN MARCH 1980.  IN JUNE OF 1980, JOHN G.
       HUTCHINSON WAS ELECTED TO SUCCEED SLACK AND RAN FOR
       RE-ELECTION IN NOVEMBER, 1980.   SINCE SLACK HAD BEEN THE
       INCUMBENT FOR A NUMBER OF YEARS, IT WAS DECIDED THAT
       QUESTIONS ABOUT THE INCUMBENT WOULD REFER TO SLACK AND
       QUESTIONS CONCERNING THE DEMOCRATIC CANDIDATE WOULD REFER TO
       HUTCHINSON.

       SPECIAL INFORMATION: LOUISIANA

          ACCORDING TO LOUISIANA  ELECTION LAWS, ANY CANDIDATE
       RECEIVING OVER FIFTY PERCENT OF THE PRIMARY VOTE IS ELECTED
       TO OFFICE AND DOES NOT APPEAR ON THE BALLOT IN THE NOVEMBER
       GENERAL ELECTION.  IN BOTH CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICTS, LA03 AND
       LA07, THE CONGRESSIONAL CANDIDATES WERE SO ELECTED.  THESE
       RACES WERE CODED 14 IN Q.AA5 (REF. NO. 740), IDENTIFYING
       THEM AS DISTRICTS WITH CONGRESSIONAL INCUMBENTS RUNNING
       UNOPPOSED. THE LOUISIANA RACE FOR U.S. SENATE WAS DETERMINED
       IN THE SAME MANNER AND WAS CODED 14 IN Q.AA6 (REF. NO. 741).
       THE TWO CONGRESSIONAL CANDIDATES AND THE SENATE CANDIDATE
       ARE ALL REFERRED TO AS "DEMOCRATIC INCUMBENTS RUNNING

The following example from the 1948 NES study provides the standard 
format for codebook variable documentation. 

Note that NES studies which are not part of the Time-Series usually
omit marginals and the descriptive content in lines 2-5 (except for
variable name).


Line

1  ==============================

7                  Q. 17.  (IF R DID NOT VOTE)  WERE YOU REGISTERED (ELIGIBLE)
8                  TO VOTE.                                                   
9                  ...........................................................
10

13                                                                             
14             0       8.  DK                                                 
15             9       9.  NA                                                 
16           422       0.  INAP., R VOTED                                     
                                                                            


Line 2 - VARIABLE NAME.  Note that in the codebook the variable name
         (usually a 'number') does not include the "V" prefix which is 
         used in the release SAS and SPSS data definition files
         (.sas and .sps files) for all variables including those
         which do not have 'number' names.  For example the variable
         "VERSION" in the codebook is "VVERSION" in the data definition
         files.

Line 2 - "NAME".  This is the variable label used in the SAS and SPSS
         data definition files (.sas and .sps files).  Some codebooks 
         exclude this.

Line 3 - COLUMNS.  Columns in the ASCII data file (.dat file).

Line 4 - CHARACTER OR NUMERIC.  If numeric and the variable is a decimal
         rather than integer variable, the numer of decimal places is 
         also indicated (e.g. "NUMERIC  DEC 4")

Line 5 - Values which are assigned to missing by default in the Study's
         SAS and and SPSS data definition files (.sas and .sps files).

Line 7 - Actual question text for survey variables or a description of 
         non-survey variables (for example, congressional district).
         Survey items usually include the question number (for example
         "B1a.") from the Study questionnaire; beginning in 1996 
         non-survey items also have unique item numbers (for example
         "CSheet.1").

Line 9 - A dashed or dotted line usually separates question text from
         any other documentation which follows.

Line 10- When present, annotation provided by Study staff is presented
         below the question text/description and preceding code values.

Lines 11-16
         Code values are listed with descriptive labels.  Valid codes
         (those not having 'missing' status in line 5) are presented
         first, followed by the values described in line 5.  For
         continuous variables, one line may appear providing the range
         of possible values.  A blank line usually separates the 'valid'
         and 'missing' values.

Lines 11-16
         Marginals are usually provided for discrete variables.  The
         counts may be unweighted or weighted; check the Study codebook
         introductory text to determine weight usage.
